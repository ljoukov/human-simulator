Assumptions and sensitivity

We train a nearest-neighbor classifier on 768 virtual bodies. Each held-out body has both imposed changes. Intervals resample these body pairs. Both tested movement rules encode a link between muscle capacity and trunk lean. The default rule balances trunk lean against muscle effort. The second makes the body lean to preserve a torque reserve. The trunk measurement noise assumes a 3° RMS error.

Combining walking and loaded stance with all three angles scored 68.4%, compared with 68.2% for loaded stance alone under the original controller. Clinical diagnoses and pain were outside this comparison.

Proposed human experiments

When would the whole body earn its place?

The prediction to test is whether explicitly modeling compensation elsewhere in the body improves a decision for someone new.

Can we predict which gait unloads the knee?

Compare recorded gait changes in additional implant recipients. Give a regression, a local knee model and a coupled body model the same available measurements. Set both models’ parameters using training data, then predict the direction and size of load change on unseen people and gait conditions.

Independent check · implant force from repeated ordinary and modified stances. Report sign errors, magnitude errors and the comparison with ordinary step-to-step variation.

Can a second movement distinguish hip mechanisms?

Capture hip angles and trunk lean during walking and a standardized loaded stance in the same people. Independently measure hip abductor strength and passive hip-extension torque with clinicians blinded to movement predictions.

Independent check · on held-out people, first compare hip-only observations with added trunk lean. Then compare a predictor using those features directly, a mechanically uncoupled model and a coupled model given identical inputs. A claim about symptoms also requires measured symptom outcomes.

90-second presenter notes
  1. 0–20 seconds. “Is this virtual hip weak, or does it resist extension?” Point to the load line, then reveal the lever calculation. With the same lean, the shorter lever lowers turning demand by a median 7.7% at peak load.
  2. 20–42 seconds. Choose loaded stance. Reveal the two observers’ scores, 60.9% and 68.2%. “Adding trunk lean helps identify which change we imposed in these virtual bodies. The controller already lets the trunk lean in response to weakness.”
  3. 42–68 seconds. Point to the separate sample of 48 women. Hip and trunk motion in ordinary walking predicted pain less accurately than the training mean. On the knee screen, switch the ordinary reference. The same bouncy-walking peaks change from above to below the reference.
  4. 68–90 seconds. “Now test this in people. Measure strength and passive resistance independently and blind the assessors. For knee load, hold out repeated gait trials from new implant recipients and compare a regression, local mechanics and coupled mechanics on the same inputs.”
