# Hip mechanics

Can two people walk alike while the tendons and joint at the hip carry different loads? This workbench connects whole-body motion, detailed hip anatomy, public clinical measurements, and two numerical experiments. It runs with bundled motion and simulation outputs. Prepare the instrumented recordings locally to replay them and compare modeled loads with the implant measurements.

## Open the workbench

From the repository root, run the viewer and open [Hip mechanics](http://127.0.0.1:5180/hip-study/).

```sh
cd upper-limb
npm ci
npm run dev -- --port 5180
```

- **Motion lab.** Scrub one timeline to see the full body, the outer hip, the deep gluteal compartment, and the articular surfaces at the same instant. Each pane has independent camera and layer controls and can expand for closer inspection. Compare the superficial iliotibial tract with the underlying bursae, then seat or separate the joint to see the femoral head and acetabular labrum. Replay a preoperative osteoarthritis recording or four archived predictive walks. The local instrumented data add a walk with measured and modeled joint loads and a single-leg stand. [Motion sources and reconstruction](MOTION.md)
- **Mechanism search.** Compare two interventions in the same virtual body. Switch from walking to the loaded single-leg stance, then change the chart from hip flexion to trunk lean or abductor force. The example is selected for its distinctive response to the challenge. The accompanying population scores use all 256 held-out virtual anatomies. [Forward equations and experimental design](forward/README.md)
- **Implant validation.** Prepare twenty synchronized walks and single-leg stands locally to compare the measured joint force with model predictions and a fitted regression. [Local setup and model reproduction](compute/README.md#reproduce-the-study) · [Signal preparation and force-unit audit](IMPLANT_METHODS.md)
- **Gait evidence.** Inspect individual GTPS and OA gait records, scrub a real video-derived landmark track against optical motion capture, and explore how pose changes hip muscle leverage. [GTPS and OA comparison](GTPS.md) · [Video audit](data/video-audit.json) · [OpenSim geometry](data/geometry.json)
- **GPU benchmark.** Inspect scaling, agreement across devices, transfer costs and the effect of increasing the search from 4,096 to 1,048,576 candidate parameter sets. [Benchmarks and reproduction](compute/README.md)

The normal 2×2 grid is one viewport tall, with a 420-pixel minimum. Scrolling leaves its size unchanged. The top bar contains the motion source, view selector, playback controls and **Full screen**. In fullscreen the same bar stays above the anatomy, so you can change recordings or scrub all four views together. Use **Layers** in any pane to change the dissection. Press **Esc** or **Exit full screen** to return.

To compare the two anatomical regions, pause the walk on one leg. In **Deep gluteal tissues**, switch gluteus medius on and off to see how it covers gluteus minimus and its insertion. In **Articular surfaces**, switch between the seated joint and the exploded view to locate the cartilage on the femoral head and inside the socket. The full-body pane keeps the same stance visible throughout.

## What the experiments found

**The real patient groups overlapped.** We compared frontal hip excursion during the middle 60% of stance in public records from women with GTPS and women awaiting hip replacement for OA. Mean excursion was 4.23° across 51 GTPS baseline records and 2.52° across 24 OA participants. The interval from 1.99° to 4.06° contained 24 of the GTPS records and 11 of the OA participants. Using the entire stance changed the mean difference from 1.72° to 6.07°. Diagnosis coincided with laboratory, the labs used different motion models, and the OA group was older. The GTPS deposit contains 51 identifiers while its publication reports 50 women. These comparisons describe overlap and sensitivity to the measurement protocol. [Cohort derivation and uncertainty](GTPS.md)

**One ordinary video recovered timing more accurately than amplitude.** On one rear-view stride from a preoperative OA participant, MediaPipe thigh inclination tracked the projected optical reference (r = 0.918, 72/72 valid frames). Its excursion was 12.10° versus 6.54° in projection. Its absolute error averaged 5.69°, compared with 4.74° for holding the registration-period tilt constant. The reference camera and clock alignment used other frames and excluded knees. This image-plane thigh angle differs from the anatomical hip angle in the cohort comparison. [Landmarks, timing sensitivity and results](data/video-audit.json) · [Video audit code](tools/audit_video.py)

**A second movement offered more information about the simulated impairment.** We solved three coupled hip/trunk response coordinates in 1,024 virtual bodies, each with four combinations of abductor capacity and passive resistance to hip extension. Among the two isolated interventions, a classifier recovered the intervention with 62.1% balanced accuracy from walking. With camera-like angular error and an additional loaded single-leg stance it reached 68.4%, a gain of 6.25 percentage points (paired 95% bootstrap interval 1.56–10.94). Repeating the walk reached 60.5%. The data contain 8,192 primary simulations and an additional 2,560 controller and load-sensitivity trials. [Forward results](data/counterfactual.json)

**Hip contact force left the distribution of soft-tissue load unresolved.** Among the 512 best-fitting candidates, two gave joint contact waveforms only 0.017 body weights (BW) apart in RMS on the same held-out walk. At peak vertical ground force their estimates of gluteal tension differed by 0.64 BW and their estimates of inward force at the trochanteric guides differed by 0.18 BW. How much of the abductor moment passes through the band is one assumption responsible for this ambiguity. On four held-out implant recipients, mean trial error was 0.419 BW for the mechanical model and 0.274 BW for a three-coefficient regression. The central 90% model range contained only 11.3% of held-out force samples. [Model and comparison](compute/README.md)

**Faster computation enabled a direct test of a possible error source.** We shifted the assumed frontal-plane lever arm through 13 offsets from −15 to +15 mm and refitted every distinct participant bootstrap over the million candidate models. At one held-out walking phase the implant measured 2.107 BW. The lowest fifth percentile over all thirteen offsets was 2.870 BW. A systematic shift in this range was insufficient to explain that error. Across 6.30 billion candidate, resample and offset combinations, the 10-core M4 CPU took 18.12 s and the warm H100 cloud request took 1.27 s from prepared features to returned winners. All 6,006 winning candidate IDs agreed. The first request including app initialization took 10.17 s with a prebuilt cloud image. [Sensitivity results](data/bootstrap-sensitivity.json) · [Modal run](https://modal.com/apps/yavol/main/ap-eCG4CRJ4UwEHI19FaqHyOk)

The simpler scoring kernel applied the same force-balance calculation to 1,212 frames per candidate. Its largest median was 111.18 ms on the ten-core M4 CPU, 24.51 ms on the M4 GPU, and 1.93 ms on an NVIDIA H100 80 GB. The CPU and H100 returned bit-identical scores and all three recovered the same top 256 candidates. For a single seed-based scoring request, the cloud round trip took 1.15 s. The device and network measurements answer different questions. [CPU–CUDA timings](data/benchmark.json) · [Metal timings](data/benchmark-metal.json) · [Modal run](https://modal.com/apps/yavol/main/ap-uioIyPm0SsheyGRCc6DQOt)

**A change in the search algorithm made the laptop competitive.** If another candidate scores strictly better for every calibration participant, a nonnegative reweighting of those participants cannot make the dominated candidate win. We used a small set of candidates to eliminate provably dominated models before the full bootstrap search. The survivors required 277 million weighted comparisons across the offsets. Warm end-to-end times on the M4 were 2.675 s on CPU and 0.645 s on GPU. The exhaustive M4 GPU run took 1.156 s and the exhaustive H100 cloud request took 1.272 s. Every implementation reproduced the same 6,006 winners. [Metal sweep and validation](data/benchmark-metal-sensitivity.json) · [CPU pruning benchmark](data/benchmark-pareto.json)

## Relation to hip disease

Greater trochanteric pain syndrome includes several possible outer-hip disorders. Gluteal tendinopathy is one clinically studied subgroup. In a series of 877 people with greater trochanteric pain, 79.8% had no bursitis visible on ultrasound. The modeled quantities here are force, movement and muscle leverage. Tissue inflammation and pain were outside the measurements. [Long et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24147479/)

Allison and colleagues reported mean hip flexion–extension excursion in stance of 44.9° in 60 people with gluteal tendinopathy and 33.7° in 73 with hip osteoarthritis. The modeled impairments gave a mean difference of just 1.51°, compared with the published group difference of 11.3° (95% CI 9.1–13.5). Clinical validation would require both diagnoses assessed independently at the same recording sites, mixed cases, and a held-out test of the complete video pipeline. Early detection before pain would need a longitudinal cohort with later clinical outcomes. [Clinical comparison, Table 2](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/a6d57c6f-31a4-42ef-b226-9dab1fed15dd/content) · [GTPS workbook](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/66mcwyk854/1) · [Bertaux gait archive](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01483-3)

The detail panes share 76 atlas structures. The full-body view uses a separate registered skeleton and 32 bilateral soft-tissue surfaces. The right hip atlas is mirrored for left-sided recordings. Gluteal insertion shading, illustrative muscle fibres, and the optional visual separation of the joint are display conventions. A surface's movement between bones is inferred from attachments and pose. Each clip identifies the source of its trajectory. Recovering individual tissue strain or inflammation from these images would require additional evidence.

**IT-band travel in the deep view** follows material points on the iliotibial tract relative to the gluteus maximus trochanteric bursa. The reference is the contact-corrected neutral pose. We pair 237 samples on 79 outward-facing bursal triangles to their closest points on the corrected neutral band. As the hip moves, the readout uses the corrected band vertices displayed by the renderer. It tracks the motion of those same material points along the bursal surface and reports root-mean-square tangential displacement, weighted by projected area. Select **IT band travel** in the Motion lab’s phase chart to inspect any of the seven full waveforms. Each clip supplies 101 poses. For the chart, the solver evaluates 1,001 phases with linearly interpolated angles. The moving dot and deep-view readout share the value evaluated at the displayed pose.

For the four simulated hip-capacity walks, the deep pane also shows the 100% capacity walk at the same percentage of its right stride. The difference is selected travel minus reference travel. At exactly 62% of the stride the 10% and 100% capacity runs give 4.5 and 2.3 mm, a difference of +2.2 mm. This reference comes from the same walking simulation at the same speed. In the reduced-capacity walks, **100% reference ghost** overlays the reference positions of the proximal femur, deep muscles and bursae in teal. Both poses share the hip center and pelvis frame. Toggle the ghost in the deep pane to inspect the difference at any phase or camera angle. The recorded walks and single-leg stand have no matched reference in this experiment. [Motion protocols](MOTION.md) · [Travel traces](data/bursa-mechanics.json)

The original neutral atlas had 44.4% of the sampled lateral bursal area inside the band. In the corrected neutral geometry none of the 237 samples fell inside. A finer check with 948 samples gave the same finding. The geometry constraint describes the modeled layer order. It supplies no measured pressure, friction, strain or inflammation. Anatomical dissections also show that bursa number and position vary across people. [Cadaveric morphology study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18245587/)

The corrected model gave 11.24 mm of travel at isolated 20° adduction, versus 11.52 mm with the original attachment-only motion. At 30° flexion the values were 7.94 mm and 7.67 mm, respectively. The preoperative OA walking replay ranged from 8.16 to 12.15 mm in the corrected atlas. Four times as many bursal samples changed the travel estimate by less than 0.003 mm over 715 replay frames and standardized poses. Two nearly identical pose pairs previously triggered modeled travel jumps of 0.118 and 0.074 mm when the solver switched a displacement bound on or off at a mesh vertex. The revised contact bounds reduced both changes below 0.000001 mm in those tests.
