# Mechanics of the paired gait comparison

Each run compares the same virtual person, at the same movement phase, before and after changing right hip-abductor capacity or the slack length of an effective right iliotibial-band cable. The live model estimates a change around a reference movement.

## Reference and degrees of freedom

The walking target uses the 100-sample mean of eleven trials from one adult walking at 1.33 m/s. The recording supplies joint-angle estimates, trunk sway, right vertical ground reaction force and a right hip moment obtained by inverse dynamics. The opposite limbs follow the same target half a stride later. The weight-transfer demonstration instead uses a designed 2.2-second transfer from equal support onto the right leg and back. Virtual stature and mass set length, inertia, force and timing scales. The demographic labels identify the profiles. [Falisse et al.](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2019.0402) · [Pinned model and recording](https://github.com/antoinefalisse/3dpredictsim/tree/5fa3929fa9ba29d5c057c16b18671a017d8f15c1)

The live perturbation has 16 degrees of freedom. They cover pelvic list, global trunk lean, and bilateral hip flexion and adduction, knee flexion, ankle dorsiflexion, shoulder flexion and adduction, and elbow flexion. OpenSim's Simbody subsystem supplies the inertia of the source model's 20 bodies and 37 mobilities. The generator projects the mass matrix onto these 16 rotations at every reference phase. This preserves cross-body inertial coupling, including the effects of the arms and contralateral leg. Each virtual person uses a scaled version of these matrices.

## Interventions and forces

- The abductor control scales an available group torque. Two assumed strategies use the same torque capacity and trunk-lean cost. The effort strategy minimizes a weighted sum of squared active effort and squared trunk lean. The reserve strategy penalizes only demand above the remaining torque capacity, allowing ordinary posture while that reserve suffices. At the changed pose, the trunk's gravitational lever arm changes hip-moment demand. Residual hip torque and the trunk-control torque enter the multibody equations. A fixed, stature-scaled 52 mm group moment arm converts active torque into equivalent force. Utilization is the fraction of the available group torque recruited at each phase. For the 40-year-old profile at 75% capacity with no cable shortening, using available strength first preserves the reference stride while peak utilization rises from 74.9% to 99.9%. The alternative strategy produces up to 1.64° more stance-side lean.
- The cable spans a capacity-weighted combination of three published gluteus-maximus and tensor-fasciae-latae musculotendon paths through the band. Each time step evaluates length and its hip-flexion, hip-adduction and knee-flexion derivatives. A tension-only linear spring uses the neutral posture's path length as its assumed slack length. Shortening this length raises tension where the cable is taut. Cable force creates joint moments according to virtual work, `τ = −T ∂L/∂q`. Its 17 N/mm reference stiffness comes from ex-vivo whole-tract tension tests. The displayed force depends on these geometry, slack and stiffness assumptions. [Davis model](https://doi.org/10.51224/SportRxiv.1017) · [Birnbaum et al. stiffness measurements](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15378277/)
- A damped angular servo tracks the nominal movement. A compliant ankle-position constraint acts through a two-segment leg Jacobian, weighted by the measured vertical load and by a 7%-stride pre-contact targeting window. The ground-force waveforms and step timing remain prescribed. A run can compare hip moment or tissue tension at a given gait phase. It cannot infer pain, time to injury, a person's response to weakness, or a new footfall pattern.

The integrator solves `M(qref) δq̈ + D δq̇ + K δq = δτ` with implicit damping and stiffness. It re-evaluates muscle demand and nonlinear tissue length at the perturbed pose. Gravity and velocity-dependent multibody forces outside the explicit hip lever-arm correction are absorbed into nominal tracking, and the inertia matrix is evaluated along the reference trajectory. Other displayed anatomical structures provide context for this reduced-order calculation.

## Numerical checks

Run `node --test upper-limb/presentation/dynamics.test.js` from the project root using a Node version supporting JSON import attributes.

- Returning to 100% capacity and zero shortening reproduces all baseline angles and load readouts exactly.
- Across 108 combinations of profile, task, compensation strategy, capacity and shortening, doubling integration resolution from 800 to 1,600 steps per cycle changed the angles by at most 0.047°, cable tension by 0.24 N and external hip moment by 0.104 N·m. The angular discontinuity across the playback seam was at most 0.0012° after settling.
- For frames carrying at least 25% body weight on one foot, an independent nonlinear evaluation of the two-segment leg found at most 9.46 mm of ankle displacement relative to its reference hip-to-ankle vector across this sweep.
- Across 100 held-out mid-stride sample positions, interpolation of the projected mass matrix differed from direct OpenSim by at most 0.018% in relative Frobenius norm. The worst-case kinetic-energy difference across velocity directions at those positions was 0.18%.
- Across 512 independent sampled joint configurations, the cable-length fit differed from direct OpenSim geometry by 0.013 mm mean absolute error and 0.053 mm maximum. These comparisons quantify numerical agreement with the source geometry and solver. Clinical accuracy would require independent observations in people with measured impairment.

An independent nerve-block study of eight healthy men found no statistically detectable change in frontal-plane walking mechanics after acute abductor weakening. That result motivates displaying different recruitment strategies under the same strength constraint. Identifying which strategy describes an individual requires measurements of capacity and motion at matched gait events. [Pohl et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4560003/)
